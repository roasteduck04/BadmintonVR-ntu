    Where the frames come from
    
      
    For now the clip's recorded videos; later live cameras (e.g. Raspberry Pis) that send their frames
        over the network. Calibrate every camera (tab 1) for the frames it really sends. After changing the source,
        warm up again (step 1).
    
      
    
        
        
      
    
      
    
        
    Videos of 
    
        
    CameraVideo
    
        
        
    Smaller copies are made with python tools/live_run.py transcode --clip <clip> --short-side 720
          (the lab tests found 720p costs about 1 cm for little speed).
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Camera addresses
    
        
    CameraStream addressTest
    
        
    
          
          
          
        
    
        
    Any address OpenCV / FFmpeg can open: rtsp://pi1:8554/cam, http://pi1:8080/stream.mjpg,
          tcp://pi1:5000, udp://@:5000. Without cameras: python tools/fake_camera.py --clip singles_05x
          plays the videos as live cameras on http://<this PC>:8090/<camera>.mjpg. Frames are time-stamped
          when they arrive (network delay varies 10-50 ms; the warm-up estimates the camera offsets).
    
      
    
      
    
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    
      
    Court calibration
    
        Pick a camera on the left, click court points on the frame (pick the point on the court
          diagram first; 6+ points spread over the court). Save finds the field of view from the clicks and writes
          data/calib/<camera>_cam.json (the old one is archived). Below 15 px is good; the red line on
          the frame shows the worst click. Calibrate again whenever a camera moves.
    
      
    
    
    
    
      
    Camera sync (fixed cameras)
    
      
    The stream lines the cameras up in time. With sync file (best_live) it uses offsets measured
        once from the clip's offline build; with auto it estimates them in the first 2 s (1–2 frames off).
